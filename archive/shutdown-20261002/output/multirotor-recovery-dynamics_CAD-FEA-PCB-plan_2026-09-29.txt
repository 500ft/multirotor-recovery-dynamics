MULTIROTOR RECOVERY DYNAMICS — CAD, FEA AND LOGGER PCB HANDOFF
Date: 2026-09-29
Repository: 500ft/multirotor-recovery-dynamics
Planning baseline inspected: origin/main, 79f7f3b, including V995 fixture PR #41.
Owner decision: first PCB is a sensor/logger; retain the stock flight controller.
Scope: engineering plan, not released manufacturing files or a completed analysis.

1. WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

Build a whole-aircraft force measurement fixture and a removable airborne logger
for the stock Veeniix V995. The purchased load cells and acquisition modules stay
on the bench. The flying board measures motion and battery voltage and stores
time-stamped data. It does not command the stock flight controller or motors.

Keep the manufacturer battery and charging arrangement: 3.7 V, 180 mAh battery,
supplied charging cable and specified adapter. Do not add charging circuitry to
the first logger. The owner's approximately 50 g aircraft mass is an estimate;
measure flight-ready mass before assigning payload, thrust or structural limits.

The stock board's processor, firmware, signal levels and control interface remain
unidentified. A pad label or two-wire motor photograph does not establish a usable
flight-controller interface. Reserve optional expansion pads; leave them unconnected.

2. CORRECTIONS TO THE EXISTING CAD PLAN

The current cad/v995 package contains an adapter generator and conditional cradle
and base generators. The populated adapter dimensions are candidates/design
choices, not an accepted assembly. Five pending parameters prevent full generation:
aircraft_capture_width, aircraft_capture_length, cradle_wall_height,
bench_anchor_spacing and bench_anchor_hole_diameter.

A. The open U-shaped cradle needs a positive uplift restraint. A floor and side
walls locate the aircraft but do not hold it down when thrust exceeds weight.
Add a removable crossbar, strap or body-capture feature. Attach it entirely to the
loaded side of the cell. A strap attached to the bench creates a parallel force
path and invalidates the reading whenever it carries force. Do not clamp fragile
guards or rotor arms without establishing their allowable contact load.

B. Define fixed-end and loaded-end adapters separately. The candidate 80 mm cell
drawing has different M4 and M5 end threads; its association with the delivered
4540 cell is unconfirmed. The present 4.5 mm clearance-hole adapter cannot simply
be copied onto both ends. Verify the actual cell, both patterns, thread depth and
permitted mounting direction. The purchased 5 kg cell is not a drop-in substitute.

C. Current plate/wall thicknesses and the 2 mm relief are design choices. Check
load, deformation, fastener engagement and sensing-section clearance before
calling them adequate. Computational geometry tolerances are not shop tolerances.

D. Filling five missing parameters unblocks generation; it does not establish
retention, structural adequacy, manufacturing fit or measurement accuracy.

3. CAD WORK PACKAGES, IN BUILD ORDER

CAD-01 — Aircraft reference assembly and coordinate system.
Create simplified body, stock guards, battery, motor centres, rotor swept volumes,
landing contacts and connector envelopes. Model the actual configuration used in
tests. Record the body axes, capture station, rotor plane and sensor axes. Measure
flight-ready mass and estimate/measure CG using a documented method. Detailed
internal motor or stock PCB models are unnecessary; accurate envelopes suffice.
Deliver: reference assembly, dimension/evidence sheet and measured keep-outs.
FEA: none for an envelope model.

CAD-02 — Delivered load-cell reference and both end interfaces.
Record overall dimensions, fixed/loaded ends, hole coordinates, threads, usable
engagement, cable exit and sensing-section keep-out. Include screws, washers and
spacers in the model. Use separate fixed-end and loaded-end parts if needed.
Deliver: verified interface drawings and parameter rows with source/uncertainty.
FEA: adapters and joints; do not infer cell capacity from an idealised solid model.

CAD-03 — Aircraft cradle and positive retention.
Locate the fuselage repeatably without obstructing rotors, guards, battery access
or antenna. Use broad contact pads, a repeatable stop and removable retention.
Include retention hardware, contact surfaces and assembly clearance. Define the
installed preload or tightening method so repeated installation is reproducible.
Deliver: cradle, restraint, pads, fastener stack and interference review.
FEA: uplift, gravity, eccentricity, restraint bending and attachment pullout.

CAD-04 — Fixed-end pedestal, base and bench anchors.
Maintain a clear sensing section. Locate the assembly on the actual bench/clamp
pattern. Keep mounting loads out of the cell's sensing region. Include tool access,
hardware stack and anti-rotation features where required.
Deliver: base/pedestal, anchor layout, assembly drawing and installation method.
FEA: bending/stiffness and joints; hand checks for slipping and overturning.

CAD-05 — Calibration loading attachment.
Provide a repeatable load point on the intended axis. Account for attachment mass,
force direction and any pulley friction. Establish a traceable method for the
direction used in testing, including force reversal where applicable. The jig must
not touch a stationary surface and bypass the cell.
Deliver: calibration accessory and a diagram of the calibration load path.
FEA: hand calculation first; analyse any slender or highly loaded attachment.

CAD-06 — Clearance-controlled overload stops, if the cell protection assessment
requires them. Stops remain disengaged over the entire valid measurement range,
including tolerance and elastic deflection. A stop-contact event invalidates the
force record. Size the stop structure for its own specified load; do not imply
that adding a stop makes a dynamic overload safe for the cell.
Deliver: gap/tolerance stack and contact-state definition.
FEA: contact/stop load case if this option is adopted.

CAD-07 — Bench electronics tray and cable management.
Mount QT Py, NAU7802, INA260 and the relevant connectors; locate load-cell analogue
wiring away from motor power loops. Include strain relief and a flexible service
loop across moving interfaces. Characterise cable force through zero/repositioning
checks; a cable can act as an unwanted spring.
Deliver: tray/enclosure, wiring route and connector labels. Usually no FEA needed.

CAD-08 — Airborne PCB outline, mount and protection.
Co-design PCB outline, component heights and mounting points with the fuselage.
Keep mass close to the measured CG. Avoid the antenna, rotor/guard envelopes,
battery removal path and heat sources. Prefer a short, stiff, removable IMU mount;
soft foam can introduce motion that is not aircraft motion. Evaluate isolation
only after measuring the vibration problem it is intended to solve.
Deliver: board outline, STEP envelope, mount, axis marking, mass/CG budget and
connector/programming access. Check added mass including wires and retention.
FEA: mounting tabs/retention; modal analysis only if vibration evidence warrants it.

CAD-09 — Complete assembly, drawings and inspection package.
Assemble all hardware and purchased-part envelopes. Check interference, fastener
access, load path, prop clearance, cable routing and assembly sequence. Drawings
must specify datums, fits, tolerances, material and print/build orientation.
Deliver: native CAD, STEP, BOM, drawings, inspection sheet and assembly instructions.

CAD-10 — Later experimental accessories, each with a defined experiment.
Possible items: IMU orientation fixture, optical timing target/breakbeam brackets,
CG/inertia measurement jig and a controlled release fixture. Replacement guards,
impact absorbers or parachute brackets require a selected recovery mechanism and
payload allocation first. Retain the stock guards for the initial fixture/logger.

4. STRUCTURAL AND FEA PLAN

Define load cases before meshing. The force path is:
aircraft -> cradle/retention -> loaded cell end -> sensing beam -> fixed cell end
-> pedestal/base -> bench anchors.

With downward positive, a screening vertical reaction is:
Rz = (m_aircraft + m_loaded_fixture) g + F_external,z - Tz.
Include signed inertial/transient loads for the relevant case. A retention preload
internal to the aircraft/cradle system is not an additional net external load,
although it does load local parts and contacts. Taring changes the display, not the
cell's physical load. Moments follow M = sum(r cross F); an offset e gives T*e.
Approximately 50 g corresponds to 0.490 N weight, not maximum thrust.

Required load cases:
LC1: installed dead weight, motor off.
LC2: specified maximum credible steady thrust and resulting load reversal.
LC3: eccentric thrust, lateral force and torque envelope with stated sources.
LC4: restraint installation, handling and cable loads.
LC5: overload-stop contact, only if adopted and with a specified load/event.
LC6: logger mount inertia using measured or explicitly proposed acceleration.

FEA-01: loaded adapter + cradle + retention. Resolve bending, contact pressure,
joint bearing, slip and screw/insert pullout. Inspect deflection and rotor clearance.
FEA-02: pedestal/base/anchors. Resolve load-cell mount rotation and deformation,
fastener reactions, bearing and anchorage. Include bench/clamp compliance when it
affects the result. Do not fix an entire plate face that is only locally clamped.
FEA-03: logger mount. Check retention and clearance under the stated acceleration;
compare mount stress to material/process allowables. Use hand checks where adequate.
FEA-04: modal analysis, conditional. Use realistic mass and constraints; compare
with measured excitation. Blade passage frequency is blade count * RPM / 60.
Motor RPM and structural damping must be measured or bracketed. A modal model alone
does not establish force measurement bandwidth or an isolation benefit.
FEA-05: impact protection, deferred. Impact involves contact, deformation, material
rate effects and possibly failure. Linear static FEA cannot certify a crash design.

For each structural study deliver:
- Geometry revision, material/process evidence, units, contacts, joints and loads.
- Hand calculation for the dominant load path and order of magnitude.
- At least three systematically refined meshes in the critical region.
- Proposed numerical checks: reaction imbalance <=1% of applied force/moment
  scales, and <5% change in the chosen convergent displacement/stress metric over
  the final two meshes. These are numerical checks, not physical acceptance limits.
- Investigate singular corner/contact peaks; do not use a non-convergent maximum
  nodal stress as a strength claim.
- Separate strength, stiffness, joint integrity and measurement error conclusions.
- Explicit allowable loads, safety factors and clearance/rotation budgets with
  their sources. Unknown limits remain unresolved, rather than receiving PASS.
- For printed parts, process/orientation evidence and coupon or component checks;
  an isotropic bulk material value is only a screening assumption.

Follow 500ft/engineering-audit/docs/cad_agent_briefing.md: parameterised native
SOLIDWORKS features, independent geometry oracle, parameter re-drive, reopen and
STEP round-trip checks. Keep the project parameter register canonical. The briefing
demonstrates a linear-static FEA route; automated assemblies, drawings and a fully
independent FEA oracle are not established capabilities. Plan manual review where
automation is incomplete.

5. AIRBORNE LOGGER PCB — REQUIRED FUNCTIONS AND CANDIDATE PARTS

This is a Rev A architecture. Component families below are researched candidates;
package, footprint, passives and final procurement BOM require schematic review.

U1: RP2040 MCU. Reuses experience with the purchased QT Py. Needs the complete
Raspberry Pi reference support circuit: boot QSPI flash, clock network, power and
core-regulator decoupling, reset/boot provisions, SWD and USB support if used.
Select boot flash from the hardware guide's supported configurations. A small
dedicated boot device plus separate log storage makes timing behaviour clearer.

U2: LSM6DSOX six-axis IMU candidate. Provides acceleration AND angular velocity.
The bought LIS3DH is acceleration-only and cannot directly measure body rates.
Use SPI, a data-ready interrupt and sensor FIFO. Supply VDD/VDDIO at a supported
3.3 V level and copy the manufacturer's decoupling/application requirements.
Place on a mechanically stable region, away from the switching regulator and
mounting stress. Mark axes on silkscreen and record the board-to-body transform.

U3: dedicated serial log flash; W25Q64JV, 64 Mbit/8 MiB, is a candidate.
Use a compatible 3.3 V package, local decoupling and defined control-pin states.
Pre-erase before capture and use RAM buffering; measure worst-case page programming
latency and prove that it cannot stall IMU servicing. Consider separate SPI buses
if measured contention cannot be handled with FIFO/buffers.

U4: 3.3 V regulator from the 1S battery. TPS63031 buck-boost is a candidate with a
1.8-5.5 V input range. Select the inductor, capacitors, enable network and layout
from its datasheet for the measured peak load. Switch current rating is not an
output-current guarantee. Test rail droop during motor starts and flash writes.
Set low-voltage behaviour appropriate to the battery/stock system; the regulator's
minimum input rating is not permission to discharge the battery that far.

Battery input: verified mating connector/polarity, suitable reverse-polarity
protection, local bulk capacitance and an off/enable provision. The logger branch
is parallel to the stock controller's supply. Do not route motor current through
the small logger PCB or duplicate battery charging.

Battery measurement: resistor divider + RC filter into MCU ADC. Determine divider
ratio from maximum credible battery voltage including tolerances; keep the ADC
within its input range. Account for ADC reference variation, source impedance and
off-state leakage. Calibrate against a DMM; voltage alone is not precise state of
charge. Never connect the battery directly to a 3.3 V GPIO/ADC.

Programming/download: SWDIO, SWCLK, GND, target voltage sense, RUN and BOOT access.
Prefer pads and a removable programming dock if a permanent USB connector consumes
too much payload. If USB-C is fitted, include the correct USB device resistors,
CC pull-downs, ESD protection and routing from the relevant reference design.
Define USB/battery power handling explicitly before schematic release. A simple
Rev A option is USB data only with a battery-powered target; a service supply may
power the battery-input connector only with the battery disconnected. Never allow
USB 5 V to feed the stock battery. An automatic dual-source design needs a reviewed
power-path circuit and adds components/mass.

Other required items: one low-current status LED with resistor; accessible power,
ground and bus test pads; mounting holes or a defined adhesive/retention interface;
board identity/revision and axis labels. Add a ground-referenced sync input pad for
bench work. Leave UART expansion pads unconnected to the stock controller.

Do not populate load-cell ADC, full-size purchased breakouts, motor drivers, radio,
GPS, magnetometer or barometer on Rev A without a specific measurement requirement.
An IMU cannot by itself provide reliable absolute altitude or touchdown velocity.
Add an external reference measurement if those quantities are needed for validation.

6. PROPOSED AIRBORNE CONNECTION MAP

POWER:
Stock battery + -> stock controller + (existing path retained).
Stock battery + -> protected logger branch -> U4 -> 3V3.
Stock battery - -> stock controller ground and logger ground.
3V3 -> U1 reference power network, U2 VDD/VDDIO, U3 and boot flash.
Battery + -> divider/filter -> U1 ADC input.
Manufacturer charger -> stock battery by the original charging procedure.

SIGNALS, provisional RP2040 GPIO allocation (verify package pins in schematic):
GPIO2 / SPI0 SCK -> IMU SCK and log-flash CLK.
GPIO3 / SPI0 TX  -> IMU SDI and log-flash DI.
GPIO0 / SPI0 RX  <- IMU SDO and log-flash DO.
GPIO1           -> IMU CS, inactive high by defined pull-up.
GPIO5           -> log-flash CS, inactive high by defined pull-up.
GPIO4           <- IMU INT1/data-ready.
GPIO6           -> status LED through resistor.
GPIO7           <- optional 3.3 V sync input, with defined idle state/protection.
GPIO26 / ADC0   <- battery divider/filter.
Dedicated QSPI pins -> boot flash; separate from the log-flash SPI wiring above.
Dedicated SWD pins -> programming pads.
Dedicated USB pins -> download connector/dock via reference support network.
RUN/BOOT network -> accessible programming controls per RP2040 hardware guide.

This allocation is a design proposal, not a verified routed pinout. Confirm GPIO
multiplexing, boot states, pin numbering, footprints and unpowered behaviour before
layout. Shared MISO requires both slaves to release the line while deselected.

7. REUSE THE PURCHASED HARDWARE ON THE BENCH

No second custom PCB is required to start. A bench carrier can be a later convenience.
QT Py RP2040: timestamp acquisition and send records to the host.
NAU7802: bridge excitation/readout for one selected load cell.
INA260: whole-aircraft battery voltage/current/power at a defined measurement point.
LIS3DH: bench/fixture acceleration; label location and axes, not aircraft gyro data.
IR breakbeam: event timing; establish its physical meaning and detection uncertainty.

Proposed wiring using BOARD LABELS, not raw RP2040 GPIO numbers:
- QT Py STEMMA SDA1/SCL1 -> NAU7802 SDA/SCL, 3V/GND, address 0x2A.
- QT Py side SDA/SCL -> INA260 SDA/SCL (default 0x40) and LIS3DH SDA/SCL
  (0x18 or 0x19 depending address configuration), with compatible 3.3 V logic.
- A0, configured digital input -> NAU7802 DRDY.
- A1, digital input -> breakbeam output, with a 3.3 V pull-up and common ground.
- A2, digital input -> LIS3DH INT1 if used.
- A3, digital input -> INA260 ALERT if used, with appropriate pull-up.
- Cell E+/E- -> excitation terminals; signal pair -> A+/A-. Determine actual
  wire functions from the delivered cell documentation/verification, not colour.
- Battery + -> INA260 IN+; INA260 IN- -> stock controller +.
- Battery - -> stock controller - and measurement ground as required.

IN+ and IN- are the series current path, not battery positive and negative. Power
the INA260 logic from its supported logic rail. Decide whether the logger branch
is upstream or downstream of this shunt: that determines whether its consumption
is included. Use current-rated connectors/wire, not logic jumper wires, for motors.
Measure harness/shunt voltage drop because it can alter the aircraft under test.

QT Py STEMMA and side I2C pins are different buses; configure both explicitly.
Check the aggregate pull-up resistance from all breakouts. The purchased single
STEMMA cable is not sufficient to wire the complete system without other wiring.
NAU7802 provides selectable rates up to 320 samples/s; 24-bit output does not mean
24-bit system accuracy or 1 kHz force bandwidth. Extra ADC inputs do not imply two
simultaneous conversion channels. Record the installed breakout revision.

8. DATA AND ELECTROMECHANICAL REQUIREMENTS

Record trial_id, board/firmware revision, monotonic acquisition timestamp, sequence
number, acceleration, angular rate, battery voltage, sensor range/rate configuration,
overflow/clipping flags and calibration identifier. Preserve raw values and units.
Timestamp acquisition/data-ready events; a host USB receipt time is not the sample
time. Use FIFO sample timing correctly when reading batches.

Proposed sizing example, not a frozen requirement: a 32-byte record at 1 kHz is
32 kB/s. An 8 MiB log device stores roughly 262 s before metadata/reserved space.
Select an actual supported IMU output rate and design anti-aliasing for the intended
measurement bandwidth. Test sustained capture, missed samples and write latency.

Synchronise bench and flight streams using a defined common event/timebase and
measure offset/drift. A wired sync pad is useful on the bench; it is not a wireless
flight synchronisation solution. Optical events/LED timing require a measured
latency and a reference recording method. Do not silently align unrelated clocks.

Keep a payload budget for board, components, mount, wire and retention. As a scale
example only, 5% of an actual 50 g vehicle would be 2.5 g; this is not an approved
payload allowance. Establish the allowance from measured aircraft mass, hover/thrust
margin, electrical draw and flight behaviour. Recheck CG after installation.

9. EXECUTION AND ACCEPTANCE ORDER

Step 1: capture dimensions/mass and verify cell interfaces. Record three repositioned
readings for critical dimensions plus tool resolution, datum, photographs and source.
Step 2: freeze the reference assembly and complete CAD-02 through CAD-04, including
positive retention, cell clearance and the real bench anchors.
Step 3: define signed loads/allowables and perform hand checks plus FEA-01/02.
Step 4: finish drawings, hardware stack, inspection and calibration accessory.
Step 5: prototype the logger acquisition/storage behaviour before committing its
PCB outline. Demonstrate sustained capture and six-axis measurements on the bench.
Step 6: co-design the airborne board/mount using the measured payload envelope.
Step 7: complete schematic ERC, independent pin/power review, layout DRC, footprint
checks, 3D interference check and BOM availability check before fabrication.
Step 8: bring up a manufactured board with current-limited power; verify rails,
boot/programming, IMU identity/axes, flash capture, voltage calibration and resets.
Then verify motor-on power/noise behaviour on the retained, instrumented aircraft.
Step 9: weigh the full installation, check CG/clearances and establish the limits
of the first flight measurement campaign. Do not infer recovery capability from a
successful static thrust test or logger boot.

For handoff, produce three reviewable packages: (1) fixture CAD/drawings and FEA,
(2) logger schematic/BOM/connection map, (3) board-and-mount integration plus test
results. Pending measurements remain explicit parameters. No guessed dimension,
unrun analysis or unverified hardware interface is labelled complete.
