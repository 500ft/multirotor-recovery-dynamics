LITERATURE CRITIQUE, RESEARCH AMENDMENTS AND CLAUDE HANDOFF
September 24, 2026 | Planning only

DECISION
========
Keep the instrumentation-first V995 plan. Change the scientific framing and
several evidence claims before commissioning another large simulation sweep.
The strongest direction is a measured, configuration-specific recovery boundary
with explicit sensing and altitude limits. A comparison of different aircraft
designs is useful, but it is not automatically an executable recovery policy.

Research question for this review:
What can published fault-tolerant-control, sensing and impact experiments justify
about a low-altitude recovery study on the stock, approximately 50 g Veeniix V995,
and which assumptions must be corrected before its results are interpretable?

2. EVIDENCE SCREEN
==================
Included: primary research directly addressing failed-rotor dynamics, detection,
estimation, transient recovery, identification or impact measurement. Adjacent
work is included when it challenges a specific novelty or measurement claim.
Excluded as quantitative support: marketing summaries, unexplained literature
numbers, and thresholds borrowed across vehicle classes without a transfer model.

Aboutness: 3 = core; 2 = relevant adjacent work. Evidence categories here are
claim-specific: B = controlled physical experiment; C = simulation/HIL or a
limited validation; U = relevant publication located but modality/details still
unverified. No result below is graded as independent replication on the V995.
Methods/statistics papers are assessed as methods, not forced into flight grades.

Primary-source register (URLs are the sources actually inspected):

[L1] Mueller & D'Andrea, ICRA 2014. Aboutness 3; B for one/two-opposite
propeller loss, C for three lost propellers. Read author-hosted full paper.
It establishes spinning reduced-attitude flight; the three-loss demonstration
is simulated because required thrust exceeds the experimental vehicle's limit.
The physical platform is 0.50 kg. This is not a universal recovery guarantee.
https://hiperlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/2014_StabilityAndControlOfAQuadrocopterDespiteTheCompleteLossOfOneTwoOrThreePropellers.pdf

[L2] Sun, Baert, Strack van Schijndel & de Visser, ICRA 2020, Upset Recovery
Control. Aboutness 3; B for demonstrated toss recovery, C for sampled envelope.
Read full paper. Its Monte Carlo starts at 50 m and 10 m/s horizontal speed;
one successful simulated recovery loses over 30 m. Allocation constraining an
undesirable angular-rate component improves recovery. It supports transient
controller benchmarking, not a claim that arbitrary states recover within 3 m.
https://arxiv.org/html/2002.09425

[L3] Sun et al., RA-L 2021, Frames vs. Events. Aboutness 3; B, single-platform
experiments. Read author-hosted full paper. High spin complicates both camera
tracking and attitude estimation. Their estimator accounts for centripetal
acceleration and IMU displacement. The custom controller/vision/range-sensor
system is substantially different from the V995 and LIS3DH logger.
https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/RAL21_Sun.pdf

[L4] Strack van Schijndel, Sun & de Visser, 2021 preprint / ICUAS 2023 final
record. Aboutness 3; B. Read preprint full text and final institutional record.
Sections V-B/V-C explicitly show detection-delay box plots across 26 failure
flights, including outliers, and a reported delay interval. Inputs include RPM,
gyro and acceleration. Thus a blanket claim that nobody characterizes detection
delay variability is false. Replaying those flights under many tuning settings
does not create independent new flight trials.
https://arxiv.org/html/2102.06439
https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/fast-loss-of-effectiveness-detection-on-a-quadrotor-using-onboard-2/

[L5] Yeom et al., ICUAS 2024, Experimental System Design of an Active
Fault-Tolerant Quadrotor. Aboutness 3; B. Read full preprint. It studies a 700 g
platform, including transition altitude and mechanical changes to rotational
drag. Use it to motivate a sensor/actuator/mechanics co-design comparison; its
hardware and performance numbers are not V995 design values.
https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06340v1

[L6] Cintas & Ozyer, Control Engineering Practice 169, 106779, April 2026.
Aboutness 3; U pending complete methods review. Publisher highlights explicitly
report real flight on a sub-35 g UAV; abstract reports rotor-speed reduction and
fault-tolerant hovering. Publisher search extract and author repository inspected;
full article access failed. Complete rotor stoppage versus partial degradation,
compute location, total instrumented mass and trial counts remain to be checked.
This is a mandatory counterexample to broad claims that small-scale FTC is empty;
it is not yet verified evidence of complete rotor-loss recovery at that mass.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conengprac.2026.106779
https://github.com/emrecintas/fault_tolerant_control

[L7] Siotia et al., Scientific Reports 2026, 18189. Aboutness 2; C for the cited
landing outcomes. Full publisher text read. Methods/results identify simulated
and HIL recovery tests; the cited impact speeds are not physical drop measurements.
Table 4 reports latency mean and standard deviation over 200 HIL runs. The text
separates processing/persistence/trigger delay from deployment. Do not convert
this evidence into a physically validated V995 inflation time or altitude floor.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-47045-0

[L8] Eschmann et al., Data-Driven System Identification of Quadrotors Subject
to Motor Delays, 2024. Aboutness 2; B. Full text read, including identification
procedure. A fitted Crazyflie motor time constant is 0.072 s. It is a model
parameter for that system, not a measured V995 dead time; model structure matters.
https://arxiv.org/html/2404.07837v2

[L9] Mili et al., From Bench to Flight, 2026 preprint. Aboutness 2; B for the
reported controlled impacts, not replicated V995 evidence. Full text read.
Their impact apparatus uses 6250 Hz analog acquisition and 1000 fps video,
measuring force and rebound. This supports distinguishing contact dynamics from
steady thrust measurement. Do not transfer their force limits or percentage
improvements to this vehicle, or treat the preprint as a certification standard.
https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05922v1

[L10] Briod et al., A Collision-resilient Flying Robot, JFR 2014. Aboutness 2;
B for the reported demonstrator, abstract/institutional description inspected.
A protective cage coupled through a passive three-axis gimbal reduces transfer
of collision-induced friction torques. This mechanism is not equivalent to rigid
propeller rings. Benefits must be tied to actual construction and contact mode.
https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.21495

[L11] Fagerland, Lydersen & Laake, 2013 and 2014 paired-proportion methods.
Core methodological relevance. Primary abstracts inspected. McNemar comparisons
address discordant paired outcomes; effect-size intervals are a separate choice.
Mid-p improves power in their evaluation but lacks a universal exact-level
guarantee. These papers do not make independently sampled simulations invalid.
https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-91
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24648355/

[L12] Sanchez-Cuevas, Heredia & Ollero, 2017, aerodynamic ground effect.
Aboutness 2; B, primary publisher article inspected. Whole-vehicle proximity
effects motivate documenting rotor-plane clearance and checking setup sensitivity.
A literature clearance ratio is not proof that this particular bench is free
of flow interference.
https://doi.org/10.1155/2017/1823056

Additional bounded leads, not fully reviewed:
Ke, Cai & Quan, T-RO 2023, doi:10.1109/TRO.2023.3297048: author abstract reports
outdoor multiple-failure experiments, including adjacent failures. Full PDF
retrieval exceeded web-tool size. Retain as a precedent; do not describe every
initial condition or hardware configuration as solved.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12972
Rotor-Failure-Aware Quadrotors Flight in Unknown Environments, 2025 preprint:
https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11306v1
Read relevant planning/experimental sections. It combines diagnosis, constrained
planning and FTC; any broad assertion that failure-aware planning is absent must
account for it. It does not by itself settle this project's multi-action question.

3. REQUIRED CHANGES, ORDERED BY CONSEQUENCE
==========================================

C01 — Separate design selection from actions available during flight. P0.
Evidence: inspected action definitions; engineering inference, high confidence.

The current realloc_only case removes guard mass/inertia, disables a control
floor and uses a different landing criterion. The mechanism case retains them.
An airborne vehicle cannot choose to have launched without its guard. Similarly,
a carried parachute's mass remains present even when it is not deployed.

Keep historical results as comparisons of complete design/control packages.
For a new operational policy, define hardware configuration c before launch and
allow only feasible actions A(c). Compare actions on the same mass, inertia,
propulsion and installed protection. Evaluate architecture selection separately:
  design comparison: compare c1 with c2 under declared policies;
  runtime policy: pi_c(observed state, uncertainty) selects an action in A(c).

Minimum amendment: list each configuration, installed devices, available actions,
required observations and actuation access. A V995 action that requires unknown
motor access is UNAVAILABLE, not merely a low-scoring action. Passive protection
is part of c; it is not a command unless a deployment mechanism actually exists.

Acceptance: every proposed runtime action can be executed by one fixed aircraft
without changing installed mass, hardware or outcome definitions mid-comparison.
No claim of a runtime policy from the existing mixed-configuration ranking.

C02 — Correct the literature review's novelty claims. P0.
Evidence: L4/L6 and the adjacent planning lead; high confidence on overstatement.

Amend claim-ledger G2, novelty-and-gaps sections 2/4, and note 01 section 6.
Replace 'empirically empty' with a search-bounded statement distinguishing partial
degradation from complete loss and separating reported from verified experiments.
Add L6 and record the unresolved details instead of claiming it proves more than
its abstract. Remove the claim that detection-delay distributions are absent.
Also remove unqualified 'never', 'nobody' and 'solved' from envelope/gap statements.

Proposed positioning:
  We investigate experimentally anchored recovery and landing-proxy boundaries
  for a specified small-quadrotor configuration, including sensing limits,
  actuator dynamics and detection delay. We distinguish design comparisons from
  implementable action selection, and test whether conclusions survive model and
  outcome uncertainty. Novelty of the complete combination remains under review.

An exact confidence-interval formula is useful reporting practice; adding it to
an existing controller does not alone establish a substantive research advance.
Do not claim a 'first' this week. Keep a dated search log and a closest-work table.

C03 — Repair parachute evidence provenance; do not tune another constant yet. P0.
Evidence: L7 plus main's source/test, high confidence.

Analysis/survivable_set.py calls the reference outcomes measured; the test
test_reproduces_published_impact_speeds calls their reproduction validation.
These descriptions need correction. Fitting a free inflation interval to a
reference model's outputs is model-to-model calibration, not independent physical
validation. A passing test preserves the chosen fit; it cannot validate reality.

The existing test also supplies terminal_m_s=3.2 from a landing outcome. The
paper's stated m=2 kg, Cd=1.2, A=1.5 m2, rho=1.225 kg/m3 instead give about
4.22 m/s from sqrt(2mg/(rho*Cd*A)). This is an unresolved interpretation mismatch,
not permission to silently replace a published value or tune until agreement.

Keep the historical inflation revision and its result, labelled conditional on
assumed timing/drag. Withdraw the universal 10–15 m floor and the claim that
transferring the fitted delay to a smaller craft is proven conservative. Prescribe
separate failure, trigger, deployment-start and full-drag timestamps before any
future calibration. The current parachute branch does not use cell.delay_s;
therefore its timing semantics need reconciliation with the active branches.

For V995 planning: no characterized deployable system exists in the inventory.
Mark parachute action UNAVAILABLE for this configuration. A hypothetical-device
sensitivity study may remain separate; it is not a hardware capability.

C04 — Make the failure scenario and recovery metric physically consistent. P0.
Evidence: code inspection + L1/L2; high confidence on mismatch, uncertain outcome.

The simulator still cuts all motors for detection+startup delay. That is a release
startup scenario. In-flight loss requires healthy motors to retain their evolving
states and follow the nominal controller until detection/reconfiguration. Specify
whether failure is motor stop, propeller loss, effectiveness loss or power loss;
these are not identical thrust/torque/drag or inertia transients.

The generic settled detector requires norm(omega)<0.3 rad/s, incompatible with a
deliberately spinning equilibrium. It is not the landing classifier used by the
current study, so do not falsely claim it caused the current landing results.
It does mean that detector cannot be reused as a spinning-recovery success flag.

Define three different outcomes:
  (a) reduced-attitude regulation: thrust/primary-axis error within a registered
      band, bounded transverse rates, allowed spin within estimator range, held
      for a registered dwell time;
  (b) recovery before contact: (a) occurs with remaining clearance and feasible
      vertical deceleration, recording altitude consumed and lateral excursion;
  (c) landing/impact outcome: touchdown motion and separately observed damage.
Thresholds belong to a new protocol; leave proposed values explicitly undecided.

Simple scale check, not a flight prediction: drag-free time to ground from rest
is 0.553/0.782/1.106 s at 1.5/3/6 m. For downward speed w0, it is
  t_ground = (sqrt(w0^2 + 2*g*h) - w0)/g.
Real thrust, drag and initial velocity change this. The comparison explains why
an asymptotically stable controller can still fail a low-height recovery task.

C05 — Do not attribute a guard's benefit to an arbitrary controller restriction. P0.
Evidence: action definitions + mechanics inference, high confidence.

The code links inverted motor operation to guard presence. In free air, inversion
alone does not mechanically prevent an unguarded rotor from generating thrust.
Contact, obstruction or an explicit operational rule might prevent it; none is
represented by the simple quarter-collective switch itself.

Before claiming the guard enables airborne recovery, write its causal mechanism:
Does it prevent rotor contact? Preserve clearance under load? Decouple contact
torque? Absorb deformation energy? Change drag? Each requires its own evidence.
L10 supports one particular construction, not generic rigid rings.

For a successor study use the same admissible airborne controller on both
architectures; vary mass/inertia/aerodynamics/clearance explicitly. Then compare
controller-floor enabled/disabled on one fixed architecture as a diagnostic.
Label counterfactual hardware combinations and avoid physically impossible ones.
Retain historical package comparison; it cannot isolate guard causation.

C06 — Preserve bench instrumentation; add an observability contract. P1.
Evidence: purchased inventory + L3/L4; high confidence.

Write one row per desired output: quantity, sensor/source, frame, timing, range,
uncertainty and availability. Current aggregate force/current, specific force and
beam events cannot establish per-motor thrust, gyro rate, full attitude, trajectory
or failure identity. Do not implement those plots with surrogate labels.

For future attitude sensing, document sample/filter delay, per-axis gyro range,
clipping and sensor position relative to CG. Rigid-body specific force includes
alpha cross r and omega cross (omega cross r). Illustrative calculation:
20 rad/s at 10 mm perpendicular offset gives 4 m/s2 centripetal acceleration.
That is not gravity tilt. Mounting dimensions are therefore estimation inputs.

Adding an event camera is not the default next purchase. First decide whether
existing stock telemetry, external calibrated tracking, or a small logged IMU
can answer the specific experiment; each has different mass and feedback limits.
External tracking may supply ground truth without making control onboard-only.

C07 — Correct the spin-scaling argument and identify dynamics separately. P1.
Evidence: rigid-body equations; engineering derivation, high confidence.

For an illustrative single-axis model:
  Izz * dr/dt = tau_residual - c1*r - c2*abs(r)*r.
Lower Izz speeds the transient if other terms are fixed. Terminal spin instead
satisfies tau_residual = c1*r + c2*abs(r)*r; Izz cancels. A smaller aircraft does
not necessarily have higher terminal spin solely because inertia is smaller.
Geometry, residual reaction torque, drag and thrust must be scaled together.

Specify separate unknowns: inertia tensor, reaction torque/thrust relationship,
rotational drag, motor response, battery-dependent thrust and estimator limits.
Static whole-drone axial force does not identify these separately. L8 is an
analogue for choosing model form, not a V995 motor parameter to accept by default.
Distinguish pure delay from first-order lag; a first-order response reaches 63%
at one time constant and approximately 95% at three, not a delayed full step.

C08 — Treat touchdown limits as an unvalidated proxy, not airframe survival. P1.
Evidence: current criterion + L9; high confidence on missing validation.

The 2.0/2.5 m/s and 30/60 degree thresholds are assumptions. Borrowing a manned
aircraft landing-gear speed band does not validate them for a tiny plastic drone.
Human-injury metrics also do not establish whether electronics/props/arms survive.

Retain historical threshold values and report sensitivity. A new contact-state
record should include vertical and lateral velocity, attitude, angular velocity,
surface, contact location, configuration and specimen history. Store physical
damage and post-event function independently from a kinematic proxy pass.
Total pre-contact kinetic energy is
  E = 0.5*m*(vx^2+vy^2+vz^2) + 0.5*omega^T*I*omega.
Even this energy is not a universal damage criterion: load path and stopping
distance matter. At assumed 50 g, translation at 2/2.5/3 m/s gives
0.100/0.156/0.225 J; these are calculations, not tolerable-energy measurements.

Do not promote the purchased static load-cell chain into an impact instrument.
At 80 SPS, samples are 12.5 ms apart; even 320 SPS gives 3.125 ms spacing, before
filter delay. Peak force needs demonstrated bandwidth, mechanical response and
adequate samples over contact. First plan an unpowered contact/clearance study
with an appropriate surrogate and observable outcomes; powered drops remain a
different protocol. Repeated drops on one damaged specimen are not independent
production-airframe trials. A one-afternoon pilot cannot validate reliability.

C09 — Improve statistics without overstating what pairing proves. P1.
Evidence: L11 plus candidate a083e80, high confidence on interpretation.

Keep useful candidate work: paired tables, discordant counts, explicit
no_discordant_pairs, historical criteria preserved. Correct these interpretations:
  - Independent Monte Carlo draws are a valid design. They are not 'paired by
    construction' merely because both actions sample the same distribution.
  - Common random numbers can improve precision, depending on covariance. They
    do not retroactively prove earlier overlap was manufactured.
  - For estimates A/B, Var(A-B)=Var(A)+Var(B)-2*Cov(A,B). State the coupling.
  - McNemar's conditional win proportion among discordant trials is not the
    unconditional landing-success improvement. Report Delta=(n10-n01)/N too.
  - Zero discordant observations does not prove equivalent true probabilities.
  - Two one-sided 95% limits on ONE proportion form an at-least-90% central exact
    interval. Limits belonging to TWO DIFFERENT actions do not constitute a
    central interval for their difference. Amend claim-ledger E1 accordingly.

Check physical trial identity, not just equal seeds: shared nuisance draws and
noise at common physical times; action-specific random streams kept separate;
pairing through chunk merging verified. Don't pair parachute RNG draws with
vehicle draws merely because their seed prefixes match.

The candidate A/A2 comparison still changes controller and drag simultaneously.
Better pairing cannot identify a feedforward effect from that bundled contrast.
The 192 comparisons also reuse related outcomes across criteria/cells; pooled
significance is not evidence from 192 independent physical experiments. Keep
pooling descriptive, report per-cell effects and address multiplicity prospectively.

C10 — Bound what 'the boundary' means. P1.
Evidence: study domain + engineering inference; high confidence.

The current map is conditional on limited tilt/tumble axes, failure location,
assumed parameter distributions and a landing proxy. Monte Carlo confidence
bounds quantify finite sampling under that model, not model error or real-world
vehicle reliability. More samples shrink only the first of those uncertainties.

Separate observational variation (e.g. repeated noisy readings), specimen/condition
variation (e.g. battery state), and unknown parameters/model choices. Until data
support a probability distribution for an unknown parameter, report a scenario
bracket rather than silently declaring it uniformly distributed.

An executable selector observes estimates, not true simulation state or the exact
instant a hidden failure began. Include state uncertainty and diagnosis confidence;
evaluate across plausible hypotheses or abstain when action preference changes.
Retain an oracle policy only as a labelled performance upper benchmark.
Do not infer the failure class or actual detection delay from a single beam.

4. DEVELOPMENT PROPOSALS WORTH KEEPING
=====================================
These are project proposals, not claims of unoccupied literature.

P1. Separate boundaries for estimation, actuation, altitude and contact.
Output, for each trial: controller version, scenario, sensor clipping/staleness,
allocation residual/saturation, regulation time, maximum descent, ground-contact
state and outcome status. A failed landing should have a traceable cause rather
than only a binary label. Classify simultaneous causes without forcing a unique
root cause. This tells the next bench measurement what uncertainty to reduce.

P2. A 'minimum required height' comparison with uncertainty.
For fixed configuration, initial motion and executable policy, estimate height
needed to meet the declared proxy. Retain the existing sparse grid as historical.
Use additional heights to check whether success is monotone before proposing
bisection; contact/controller timing can invalidate that assumption. Independent
held-out conditions should test an estimated boundary, not only its interior.

P3. Measurement priorities based on whether they change the decision.
For each uncertain input, vary it over a physically justified bracket and count
which predeclared conclusions/actions change. Prioritize measurements that resolve
those reversals. Do not assign a prior distribution solely to compute a polished
expected-value score. Start with control access, usable force resolution and
mounted mass; these determine whether a dynamic experiment is possible at all.

P4. Benchmark before controller innovation.
Once scenario/allocation/timing are coherent, specify reproduction of one
published reduced-attitude reference in its stated regime. Compare on identical
plant/observations/limits against the existing controller. Use matched seeds for
precision, then independently sampled held-out cases for robustness. Add new
controllers only after the benchmark works or its transfer failure is explained.
Do not start RL, onboard vision or a full NMPC stack to compensate for missing
motor authority or state measurements.

6. CAD, VALIDATION AND REPORTING BOUNDARIES
=========================================
For every future cradle, sensor mount or guard, read:
  500ft/engineering-audit -> docs/cad_agent_briefing.md
  https://github.com/500ft/engineering-audit/blob/main/docs/cad_agent_briefing.md
Apply its independent geometry oracle, parameter re-drive and STEP round-trip
workflow. Critical interfaces need position/clearance checks; equal volume alone
does not validate them. Keep accepted inputs in this project's canonical register.

Geometry validation, static FEA, impact prediction and physical survival are
different evidence levels. A linear-elastic static solve does not establish
impact absorption, buckling, contact duration, damping or fracture. Use surrogate
coupons/load-path tests before proposing destructive complete-aircraft trials.
Do not import RoboRacer mast geometry or constraints from the shared briefing.

For any later physical reliability claim, plan independent experimental units,
condition coverage, uncertainty and stopping rules before collecting the decisive
data. Illustration: zero failures in 59 independent, identically distributed
trials yields a one-sided exact 95% lower success bound above 0.95. Ten successes
or repeatedly dropping one progressively damaged vehicle do not establish that
claim. This calculation is not a demand for 59 drops this week.
