VEENIIX V995 — CONTROL-BOARD RESEARCH AND NEXT INVESTIGATION
Prepared: September 21, 2026
Project: 500ft/multirotor-recovery-dynamics
Scope: desk research and visual inspection; no electrical probing or firmware work.

RESULT
======

There are useful board-level leads, but the MCU, inertial sensor and programming
protocol are not identified reliably yet. The most useful next evidence is clear
chip marking and underside photography, followed by a documented pad map.

Owner decision: use the manufacturer charger specification, 5 V DC / 2 A adapter
input with the supplied charging cable, and the listed 3.7 V / 180 mAh flight
battery specification. The weekly plan no longer asks to reconfirm these labels.

DIRECT OBSERVATIONS FROM THE OWNER'S PHOTO
==========================================

Source: /Users/redhose/Downloads/IMG_3883.HEIC. Inspected the full photo and a
cropped/rotated viewing copy; no synthetic sharpening or generated detail used.

1. A compact central PCB has the motor leads soldered directly to it. Two-wire
   connections support a brushed-motor interpretation, pending electrical/part
   identification. The exact switching devices and motor topology are not mapped.

2. A square IC and an adjacent metal package are visible near the middle. Their
   roles and part numbers cannot be established confidently from this image.
   In particular, do not call the visible square IC the flight MCU merely because
   it is prominent; it could be another subsystem. The board's underside may
   contain the controller, RF circuitry or inertial sensor.

3. Near the power-switch end are small exposed circular pads. The marking CLK
   appears legible; nearby markings are partly obstructed. This is a useful
   candidate factory test/programming interface. It does not establish SWD, JTAG,
   UART, I2C, voltage level, firmware read access or an external control API.

4. The board edge carries an A20-containing marking and a date-like string that
   appears to read 23-05-06. Some characters are obscured. Preserve these only as
   partial visual search leads, not an accepted full board part number/revision.

5. The supplied photo shows one populated side. It does not establish a complete
   component list, exposed gyro bus or sensor supply rail.

DOCUMENTARY LEADS
=================

A. Veeniix describes V995 stock remote-control functions including altitude hold,
   headless mode and automatic maneuvers. Those functions show that control logic
   exists, but do not establish that custom firmware or external sensor integration
   is supported. The manufacturer download page inspected lists apps for other
   product families; it did not provide a V995 SDK, schematic or firmware image.
   Sources:
   https://veeniix.com/toys/V995?product_id=22&title=Products
   https://www.veeniix.com/support/downloads

B. The V995 manual's hosting/index page associates it with U46C-022. UDIRC's own
   catalog has a Firefly U46C, and CML's own parts catalog lists a U46C receiver
   board under UDI-I22-07. This is a POSSIBLE related-platform search path.
   It is not proof that the V995 uses that exact board, radio protocol or firmware.
   No replacement-board interchangeability is established; no purchase proposed.
   Primary UDIRC product and CML catalog sources:
   https://b2b.udirc.com/portfolio/u46c/
   https://www.cmldistribution.co.uk/product/UDI-I22-07/udi-rc-u46c-receiver-board
   Index lead, not independent proof of OEM identity:
   https://device.report/manual/20701008

C. The index also associates FCC ID 2BBLM-V995. Attempts to obtain a corresponding
   internal-photo exhibit through public mirrors did not yield an accessible
   exhibit. Do not claim an FCC teardown or schematic was inspected. The manual
   PDF's extracted text includes V995 and IC 33721-V995 identifiers. PDF page
   screenshots failed, and a local fetch returned HTML rather than a usable PDF.
   Thus this research does not claim a complete visual inspection of that manual.

D. Searches for V995/U46C firmware, MCU identity, board markings and interfaces
   did not yield a verified matching open firmware target or board schematic.
   This is a search result with limits, not proof that the board cannot be changed.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PROJECT
==============================

The V995 can remain the stock physical test article while QT Py independently
collects measurements. Connecting additional sensors to QT Py does not make the
V995 controller consume those measurements. Controlled recovery needs a demonstrated
path from measured state to a command the aircraft actually accepts.

Three possible development paths, to choose after identification:
  1. Independent instrumentation: preserve stock control and collect experimental
     evidence. This is the currently supported planning path.
  2. Stock-board integration: feasible only if an actual telemetry/control/programming
     interface, logic levels and software support are established. Read-only
     telemetry and command authority are separate capabilities.
  3. Controller replacement: a later design decision involving motor drivers, IMU,
     radio/override, power and mass. It is a substantial vehicle revision, not a
     simple sensor installation or an already authorized implementation.

NEXT BOARD TASKS — PROPOSED 90-MINUTE IDENTIFICATION PASS
=======================================================

B01 / Owner / about 15 minutes
  Capture sharply focused, perpendicular close-ups of the central IC, pad group,
  full board marking and the underside if accessible without forcing connections.
  Keep the original prop/motor positions and wire routing recorded. Transcribe
  every IC line and logo exactly, using question marks for unreadable characters.
  Desired result: actual markings instead of guesses from an enlarged overview.

B02 / Claude / about 30 minutes after readable markings arrive
  Match the exact marking/package to manufacturer data sheets. Separate MCU,
  RF device, inertial sensor, pressure sensor if present, regulator and drivers.
  Cross-check package/pin count and surrounding circuit; a similar search string
  is not a component identification. Bound the search instead of spending the
  entire week looking for undocumented firmware.

B03 / Owner with appropriate tools + Claude review / about 30 minutes
  Produce a power-off continuity map for known ground, battery input, exposed pads
  and identified chip pins. Label observations separately from inferred nets.
  Powered rail/logic measurements are a subsequent defined operation after the
  relevant terminals and motor behavior are understood. Do not attach a programmer
  based solely on the letters CLK. Preserve stock firmware and operation.

B04 / Claude / about 15 minutes
  Return a capability decision: component identities, candidate interface, source
  pinout, measured voltage if available, access status and smallest next test.
  If unresolved, finish independent logger work; report precisely which chip/net
  prevents controller integration. Do not present unknown access as impossible.

DELIVERABLE
===========

A board map with photographs/markings, component roles, source links, pad/net
evidence and the resulting choice among the three development paths. No confirmed
schematic, flashable target or direct sensor interface is claimed by this note.
